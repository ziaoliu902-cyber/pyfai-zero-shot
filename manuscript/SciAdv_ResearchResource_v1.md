**Short Title:** Zero-prior 2D powder X-ray detector calibration

**Teaser:** A phase-blind, zero-prior engine calibrates full-ring and corner-arc 2D X-ray detectors and defines single-frame limits.

---

## Abstract

Quantitative azimuthal integration of two-dimensional X-ray powder diffraction patterns requires accurate sample-to-detector distance, beam center, and detector tilts, yet standard calibration workflows still depend on user-supplied calibrant identities and initial geometries. Here we present `pyFAI-ZeroShot` (frozen commit `f608e67`), an open-source, phase-blind, zero-prior calibration resource. Combining adaptive flat-panel preprocessing, a multi-scale canvas ladder, consensus-arbitrated seed search, regularized corner-arc tilt, and two-parameter differential Pseudo-Voigt decoupling, it exports standard `.poni` files with quantitative identifiability tiers. Across 13 PETRA III P02.1 configurations (230–2290 mm; centered and corner arcs), it achieves 13/13 `LaB6` identification and `< 0.12%` distance error (median `0.030%`, corner center `0.74 px`). It generalizes to 16/16 CLS mineral frames, ESRF `CeO2`/`Al2O3`, and `Pilatus`/`Eiger` detectors, establishes a sharp identifiability boundary at four visible reflections, and unlocks 24/24 convergence (`0.006%`, `0.11 px`) when seeding `pyFAI`.

## Introduction

Two-dimensional (2D) area detectors are the workhorse of modern synchrotron, laboratory, and high-throughput powder X-ray diffraction experiments, supporting phase mapping, operando battery and catalysis studies, high-pressure crystallography, and total-scattering pair distribution function (PDF) measurements. Extracting quantitative one-dimensional diffraction profiles $I(q)$ or $I(2\theta)$ via azimuthal integration requires accurate calibration of the five active detector geometry parameters—sample-to-detector distance $D$, orthogonal point-of-normal-incidence coordinates $(\mathrm{poni}_1, \mathrm{poni}_2)$ encoding the beam center, and detector tilt angles $(\mathrm{rot}_1, \mathrm{rot}_2)$—under the standard geometric model used across widely adopted software suites including `FIT2D`$^1$, `DAWN`$^{2,3}$, `DIOPTAS`$^4$, and `pyFAI`$^{5-7}$. Even as downstream azimuthal integration has been accelerated to millisecond streaming rates via matrix reformulations such as `MatFRAIA`$^8$ and real-time pipelines such as `SAXSDOG`$^9$, detector geometry calibration itself largely remains an interactive expert step.

Existing calibration pipelines rely on two structural priors: first, the identity (or exact $d$-spacing list) of the reference calibrant standard, and second, a sufficiently accurate initial estimate of distance and beam center. When those priors are removed, conventional ring-extraction and least-squares routines readily lock onto self-consistent pseudo-solutions—aliasing adjacent $(hkl)$ reflections onto shifted distances or collapsing onto spurious circular arcs when the beam footprint shifts away from the geometric detector center$^{6,7}$. Recent computer-vision and machine-learning efforts have automated isolated steps such as Debye–Scherrer ring clustering$^{10}$, radial-symmetry interest detection$^{11}$, or downstream sample parameter regression$^{12-14}$, yet a single-frame engine capable of solving arbitrary detector distances (spanning small-angle PDF geometries $> 2\text{ m}$ to wide-angle configurations $< 0.15\text{ m}$), unknown calibrants, and extreme off-axis corner geometries without human seeding has remained lacking.

Four coupled challenges defeat naive zero-prior calibration on real synchrotron area detectors: (i) *scale compression*, where coarse downsampling merges closely spaced high-energy inner rings below peak-resolution limits and drives misidentification across candidate phases; (ii) *beam-center displacement and detector artifacts*, where off-axis geometries, module gaps, negative analog flat-panel detector offsets, and beamstop shadows distort radial intensity profiles computed around the nominal detector center; (iii) *false-basin self-consistency*, where low-ring or harmonic calibrants (`AgBh`, dilute/high-energy `LaB6`) admit self-consistent wrong-distance solutions with low formal ring residuals; and (iv) *partial-arc radial–distance coupling*, where rings clipped to a corner quadrant (`< 35%` azimuthal coverage) exhibit exact geometric compensation between a radial center offset $\Delta \mathbf{c}$ and a shifted distance $D$, amplifying sub-pixel centering residuals by up to an order of magnitude in fitted distance.

Here we present `pyFAI-ZeroShot` (frozen evaluation commit `f608e67`), an initialization-free, phase-blind calibration framework that addresses all four challenges within a unified physics-driven pipeline requiring only an image array, pixel pitch, and X-ray wavelength (Fig. 1a). We demonstrate `100%` phase and distance recovery (`13/13` within `< 0.12%`) across a challenging PETRA III P02.1 flat-panel benchmark covering `230–2290 mm` and extreme corner geometries, validate cross-facility transfer across CLS and ESRF beamlines as well as `Pilatus` and `Eiger` detectors, map the fundamental single-frame identifiability boundary at $n_{\mathrm{vis}} = 4$ visible reflections, and demonstrate directly that seeding standard `pyFAI` with our zero-prior solution eliminates `87.5%` cold-start `pyFAI` failure while unlocking sub-`0.01%` five-parameter accuracy.

## Results

### Pipeline architecture and partial-arc decoupling

The `pyFAI-ZeroShot` engine operates through a single deterministic entry point (`solve_image`, Fig. 1a; Methods). Raw frames first undergo scale-aware negative pedestal removal (`auto_offset`)—essential for amorphous-silicon flat-panel detectors that export large negative dark pedestals (exceeding `-6 × 10^4` counts on Varex panels)—together with automatic masking of detector inter-module gaps, hot/invalid pixels, and beamstop shadows (`auto_mask`). Rather than operating on a single fixed downsampled grid, a zero-prior multi-scale canvas ladder (`R2`) begins on a `<= 520 px` working canvas and conditionally escalates to `ceil(maxdim / 2)` (capped at `3.5 Mpx`) when the coarse stage fails (`a-none`), yields high residuals with few rings (`rms > 3.5 px` and `m < 3`, `b-rms-m`), or operates under high downsampling (`s >= 5`) where an unregularized probe detects a structural distance bifurcation `|ΔD|/D >= 10%` (`c-risk`).

To survive off-axis beam centers without manual clicking, each canvas is evaluated under a Layer-0 photometric sharpness and algebraic Kåsa center guard alongside a ring-starvation detector comparing closed-form radial-symmetry (`RS`) solutions against the geometric center (`|ln(D_RS / D_geo)| > 0.46`). When geometric-center initialization is unreliable, multi-seed fallback probes (`RS` plus ray extrapolations at `1×, 2×, 3.5×`) compete through a cross-seed consensus vote before global phase arbitration ranks candidate structures by full-profile Pearson correlation (`ps` with a `0.15` survival margin), ring coverage, and subset/harmonic disambiguation rules (Methods).

Crucially, for off-axis corner geometries (`az_cov < 50%`, Fig. 1b), forcing a zero-intercept relation $r_i = D \tan(2\theta_i)$ introduces a severe systematic distance bias whenever sub-pixel centering errors $\Delta \mathbf{c}$ remain along the mean visible arc unit normal $\bar{\mathbf{u}} = \langle (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}) / r \rangle$. Because the visible arc subtends only `~19–31%` of the azimuth, radial shifts do not cancel azimuthally as they do on full `360°` rings; every ring radius shifts by nearly the same scalar offset $\delta r_0 \approx \bar{\mathbf{u}} \cdot \Delta \mathbf{c}$, inducing a relative distance bias $\Delta D / D \approx \delta r_0 / (D \langle \tan 2\theta \rangle)$ that scales inversely with diffraction angle ($\sim 7\times$ leverage at `60 keV`). Fitting the two-parameter linear differential Pseudo-Voigt model $r_i = D_{2\mathrm{p}} \tan(2\theta_i) + \delta r_0$ and projecting the recovered intercept along $\bar{\mathbf{u}}$ cleanly separates slope (distance) from radial center offset (Fig. 1b).

A four-stage stepwise ablation across all 13 blind DESY P02.1 scans quantifies the physical contribution of each module (Fig. 1c, Table 1). Starting from a single `480^2` canvas without masking (`A0`), phase identification fails on `3/13` configurations (`SDD230_center_MJ` and `SDD1000_center_VB` misassigned to `AgBh` at `+945.3%` and `+103.2%`, and `SDD300_corner_MJ` misassigned to `Quartz` at `+82.4%`). Adding the `R2` canvas ladder and `auto_mask` (`A1`, corresponding to commit `184c1aa`) eliminates all phase misidentifications (`13/13 LaB6`) and achieves `12/13 < 0.20%`, leaving residual errors on off-axis corners (worst `+1.621%` on `SDD300_corner_MJ`, corner `ΔC` median `7.21 px`). Introducing regularized high-resolution (`1440^2`) corner-arc tilt and one-parameter Pseudo-Voigt refinement (`A2`, commit `0d3e493`) compresses corner `ΔC` by `9.1×` (to `0.79 px`) and lowers worst error to `+0.421%`. Enabling the two-parameter differential Pseudo-Voigt fit (`A3`, frozen `f608e67`) removes the remaining partial-arc bias on `SDD300_corner_MJ` (`+0.421% -> -0.052%`) and `SDD350_corner_VB` (`-0.145% -> -0.066%`), bringing all `13/13` scans below `0.12%` and reducing corner centering error to `0.74 px` (a `9.7×` improvement over `A1`) at a median runtime of `26.6 s/frame` on a single CPU core.

### Blind synchrotron validation at PETRA III beamline P02.1

To test real-world robustness on high-energy synchrotron flat-panel data, we evaluated an independent calibration archive from PETRA III beamline P02.1 (DESY, Hamburg; `~59.8 keV`, $\lambda = 0.2072\text{–}0.2074\text{ Å}$). Deduplication of 14 archive packages by raw image MD5 yielded 13 unique NIST SRM 660c $\mathrm{LaB}_6$ scans: 12 recorded on a Varex XRD4343CT detector (`2880 × 2880`, `150 µm` pixels) and one on a PerkinElmer `2048 × 2048` panel (`200 µm` pixels, `SDD350_corner_VB`), spanning nominal distances from `230 mm` to `2290 mm` in both beam-centered (`n = 5`) and off-axis corner (`n = 8`, azimuthal coverage `19.4–30.6%`) configurations (Fig. 2, Table 2). Calibration was run cold-start with caches cleared before every frame under pure zero-prior Mode A (`phases=None, d_ref_mm=None`), supplying only pixel pitch and beamline wavelength.

We explicitly report the chronological evaluation trail to avoid post-hoc tuning inflation: in our initial locked blind run (`184c1aa`), one development scan (`SDD230_center_MJ`) was used to verify pedestal and scale handling while the remaining 12 scans were held out behind reference `.poni` files; `184c1aa` achieved `13/13 LaB6` identification and `12/13` scans below `0.20%` (with `SDD300_corner_MJ` at `+1.621% / 4.08 px`). Two generic, physics-based geometric upgrades—half-pixel coordinate consistency plus regularized `1440^2` corner tilt (`0d3e493`), followed by the two-parameter differential Pseudo-Voigt decoupling (`f608e67`)—were subsequently implemented without any DESY-specific constants or tuned parameters, and re-evaluated under the identical pure blind Mode A protocol.

On frozen commit `f608e67`, all **13/13 (100%)** scans converge on the true `LaB6` phase with absolute relative distance errors strictly below **`0.12%`** (`10/13 <= 0.05%`, Fig. 2c, Supplementary Table S1). Linear parity against reference `.poni` distances across `229.65–2287.75 mm` yields **$R^2 = 0.999999$** and **$\mathrm{RMSE} = 0.82\text{ mm}$** (Fig. 2a), with an overall median distance error of `0.438 mm` (`0.0303%`; mean `0.546 mm` / `0.0377%`; worst `2.488 mm` / `-0.1158%` on `SDD2150_corner_VB`). On the 12 out-of-distribution (`Locked OOD`) scans alone, median relative distance error is **`0.0281%`** (`0.0148%` on `center`, `n=4`; `0.0364%` on `corner`, `n=8`). Paired beam-center errors across the eight corner scans drop from `7.21 px` (`184c1aa`) to **`0.74 px`** (`f608e67`, Fig. 2b), matching the ground-truth centers despite only `19.4–30.6%` visible ring arcs. Repeating all eight corner scans under Mode B (providing nominal SDD `d_ref_mm`) returns **`8/8 (100%)` bitwise identical** geometries, confirming that harmonic aliasing is completely absent on these `8–12` ring configurations. Total cold-start runtime for all 13 multi-megapixel frames was `437.8 s` on CPU (`26.6 s/frame` median, including `2.46 s` median auto-masking).

### Identifiability limit and failure taxonomy on synthetic frames

To determine the fundamental geometric information boundary independent of experimental unknowns, we conducted a 100-frame synthetic parameter sweep (`Seed = 20261120`; `2048 × 2048` detector, `150 µm` pixels, $\lambda = 0.35\text{ Å}$, `LaB6`, `400–1400 mm`, randomized beam-center offsets `±60 px`, Poisson photon peak counts `200–4000`; Fig. 3, Table 3). Evaluating exact visibility—the number of ground-truth Debye–Scherrer reflections $n_{\mathrm{vis}}$ whose radii lie inside the active canvas—reveals a sharp statistical phase transition at **$n_{\mathrm{vis}} = 4$**.

Across all **41 well-posed frames ($n_{\mathrm{vis}} \ge 4$)**, the engine achieves **`41/41 (100%)` correct phase identification** and **`41/41 (100%)` distance accuracy within `<= 1.0%`**, with **`40/41 (97.56%) <= 0.20%`** (`0.0010%` median error, `0.0571%` 90th percentile, `0.2317%` worst case on one four-ring borderline frame, and `0.61 px` median center error). For frames with $n_{\mathrm{vis}} = 5\text{–}13$ ($n = 31$), error drops to `<= 0.0019%` (`0.0007%` median) across the entire group (Fig. 3b). Conversely, across the **59 under-determined frames ($n_{\mathrm{vis}} \le 3$)**, phase hit rate falls to `20/59 (33.9%)`, only `16/59 (27.12%)` reach `<= 1.0%`, and median error climbs to `25.23%` (with `0/18` passing at $n_{\mathrm{vis}} = 1$ and `4/28 = 14.3%` at $n_{\mathrm{vis}} = 2$). Three distinct failure regimes emerge: (1) *wrong-phase under-determination* ($n_{\mathrm{vis}} \le 2$, detectable via matched ring count $m$); (2) *wrong-basin local minima* within the true phase (detectable via pattern score $ps$ or split-half residual ratio `cv_ratio > 2.5`); and (3) *partial-arc compensation*, where low azimuthal coverage (`az_cov < 40%`) permits center–distance trade-offs that remain self-consistent without external priors (flagged proactively as `PARTIAL_ARC`).

### Generalization across facilities, low-symmetry minerals, and detectors

Beyond cubic calibration standards, we tested `pyFAI-ZeroShot` across three independent synchrotron testbeds (Fig. 4, Supplementary Tables S3–S4):

1. **Low-symmetry natural minerals at CLS 08B1-1 (Fig. 4a):** Four arsenic-bearing minerals (`scorodite`, `realgar`, `orpiment`, `arsenopyrite`; four frames each, `16` total) recorded on a `mar3450` image plate (`4096 × 4096`, `73.242 µm`, `18 keV`, $\lambda = 0.6888\text{ Å}$) from the public CLS archive$^{15}$ were solved cold-start using theoretical phase cards (`mineral_phases.json`) derived directly from Crystallography Open Database CIF files via *pymatgen* (no hand-entered $d$-spacings and no geometric priors). Whereas earlier prototypes exhibited basin slips on `3/16` frames requiring posterior `ps >= 0.30` filtering, frozen `f608e67` converges directly on **16/16 (100%)** frames into the true physical basin (`m = 9–15`, `ps = +0.583` to `+0.879`, all `HIGH` confidence, zero rejections, median `12.96 s/frame`). Despite starkly distinct monoclinic/orthorhombic powder patterns, all four minerals yield an internally consistent detector distance of **`246.90 ± 1.36 mm`** (`0.55%` relative SD, median `247.24 mm`, **`MAD = 0.69 mm`**).
2. **ESRF D2AM multi-distance `CeO2` and distorted `Al2O3` (Fig. 4b):** On the seven-distance `MX_ceria` series (`150–450 mm`, Pilatus 6M, `172 µm`, $\lambda = 0.972386\text{ Å}$), pure phase-blind calibration (`phases=None`) identifies **`7/7` `CeO2`** with `0.139 mm` mean absolute distance error (`0.111 mm` median, `0.376 mm` max, `26.91 s` median), while the dedicated `phase_fit` 2D/tilt mode reaches **`0.1 mm` mean and worst error** (`rot1Δ = -2.1 mrad`, `rot2Δ = -2.5 mrad` mean tilt residuals). On a 52-frame `Al2O3` series (`FReLoN` CCD with `.spline` spatial distortion, $\lambda = 0.7085\text{ Å}$), `phase_fit` automatically rejects the blank dark frame (`dark_0001.edf`), solves `51/52` frames (`MAD = 0.68 mm`, median `116.2 mm` static without distortion map), and calibrates the 51-frame mean with forward `.spline` tilt refinement to **`116.70 mm`** against the reference `116.86 mm` (`ΔD = -0.16 mm`, `rotΔ = (+2.80, +1.41) mrad`).
3. **Cross-detector `silx_zoo` benchmark (Fig. 4c):** Evaluated blindly (`phases=None`, known $\lambda$) across `Pilatus2M` (`172 µm`), `Eiger4M` (`75 µm`, two `.poni` calibrations), `Eiger9M` (`75 µm`), and `Pilatus1M` (`172 µm`), the engine hits **`5/5` ground-truth phases** (`CeO2`, `Al2O3`, `AgBh`). Relative distance errors reach **`-0.007%`** (`Pilatus2M`), **`+0.041%`** (`Eiger4M`), **`+0.311%`** (`Eiger4M_v2`), and **`+0.008%`** (`Eiger9M`). On `Pilatus1M` (`AgBh`), whose distributed header carries a placeholder wavelength `1.0000 Å` (`GT = 1583.231 mm`), the solver identifies `AgBh` (`m=8`, `ps=0.924`) at `1638.037 mm` (`+3.46%` nominal, corresponding to only `+0.14%` in the physical $\lambda \cdot D$ invariant relative to `12 keV`).

### Head-to-head comparison and complementarity with standard `pyFAI`

To benchmark automation directly against the community standard `pyFAI` (`v2025.12.1`) calibration engine$^{6,7}$, we ran a controlled 24-frame experiment combining all 13 DESY scans, all 7 `MX_ceria` scans, and the 4 physical-$\lambda$ `silx_zoo` frames (`Pilatus2M`, `Eiger4M`, `Eiger4M_v2`, `Eiger9M`; Fig. 5, Supplementary Table S5). Standard `pyFAI` was executed for two iterative rounds of `RingExtraction(max_number_of_rings=15)` and `GeometryRefinement.refine3(fix=["wavelength"])` using unweighted control points (`cps.getList()`, matching interactive `pyFAI-calib2` behavior; see Supplementary Note on SLSQP numerical instability when raw image counts are passed to `getWeightedList`). Crucially, `pyFAI` received the true calibrant name on every frame, whereas `pyFAI-ZeroShot` (`Ours`) ran phase-blind (`phases=None`).

Without an accurate starting geometry—searching `P0` across a multi-start grid `D0 ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 1.0, [2.0]} m` from detector center `(W/2, H/2)` and selecting lowest `χ^2`—automated `pyFAI` succeeds within `< 1.0%` on only **`3/24 (12.5%)` frames** (`2/13` DESY, **`0/8` DESY corners**, `1/7` `MX_ceria`, `0/4` `silx_zoo`), suffering a median distance error of **`45.81%`** and median beam-center error of **`47.04 px`** (`1871 px` on DESY, Fig. 5a–c). Only configurations whose true distance happened to sit directly next to a grid point (`SDD1000`, `SDD2100`, `ceria_300`) converged cleanly.

By contrast, `pyFAI-ZeroShot` (`Ours`) converges on **`24/24 (100%)` frames** within `< 1.0%` (`23/24 < 0.20%`) with `0.033%` median distance error and `2.44 px` median beam-center error. Moreover, when standard `pyFAI` is initialized directly from our phase-blind `.poni` prediction (`P1`), `pyFAI` converges on **`24/24 (100%)` frames** (`22/24 < 0.20%`) and achieves **`0.006%` median distance error** (`0.005%` DESY, `0.007%` `MX_ceria`) and **`0.11 px` median beam-center precision** (`0.06 px` DESY, `0.10 px` `MX_ceria`) in just `7.3 s` median refinement time (Fig. 5b, c). Thus, `pyFAI-ZeroShot` serves as the missing zero-prior front-end that unlocks `pyFAI`'s sub-pixel 5-parameter least-squares optimum unattended.

## Discussion

The experimental evidence across 230–2290 mm synchrotron geometries, four mineral structures, and five area-detector families establishes that automated 2D powder calibration fails in conventional tools primarily because of *initialization and basin-selection fragility*, not lack of local numerical precision. By pairing an initialization-free coarse cascade (`RS`, ray extrapolation, sharpness/Kåsa center guards, and consensus voting) with physics-aware geometry refinement tailored to full-ring versus partial-arc regimes, `pyFAI-ZeroShot` turns single-frame calibration into a deterministic `~13–27 s` CPU operation requiring zero manual ring picking.

Two methodological insights merit broader emphasis for computational scattering pipelines. First, *internal self-consistency alone does not guarantee correctness* under under-determined or azimuthally starved geometries. When fewer than four reflections enter the detector ($n_{\mathrm{vis}} \le 3$), or when Debye–Scherrer rings are truncated to `< 40%` azimuth in a detector corner, shifts in beam center and sample–detector distance form a continuous ridge of low-residual local minima. Our ablation demonstrates that breaking this ridge on real corner arcs (`19–31%` azimuth) requires two complementary constraints: regularized high-resolution 2D sector tilt fitting (`A2`) and the two-parameter differential Pseudo-Voigt model (`A3`) that separates $\delta r_0$ intercept from $D \tan(2\theta)$ slope before projecting the radial center residual back onto the visible arc normal (Fig. 1b,c). Second, harmonic layered or cubic sparse-ring calibrants (`AgBh`, `AgSt`, or `< 4`-ring `LaB6`) exhibit exact $D \leftrightarrow kD$ integer invariance unless an approximate distance scale `d_ref_mm` or `>= 5` distinct orders constrain the radial ratios; our confidence layer surfaces these conditions explicitly via `(m, ps, az_cov, cv_ratio)` tiers (`HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, `PARTIAL_ARC`, `LOW`) rather than silently exporting fragile solutions.

Several clear operational boundaries remain. First, single-frame phase identification assumes the true calibrant (or user-supplied CIF/$d$-list via `custom_phase.py`) is present in the active library; unknown multiphase mixtures require adding the candidate CIFs beforehand. Second, unmodeled curved detector spatial distortions (such as fiber-optic taper `.spline` fields) degrade sub-pixel center accuracy unless the `.spline` forward map is provided (`Al2O3` `.spline` vs `Eiger4M` comparisons). Third, extreme corner arcs exhibiting severe azimuthal intensity anisotropy alongside large tilts (`> 3°`) with `< 4` rings remain fundamentally unidentifiable from one static exposure and benefit from multi-frame azimuthal stacking or weak distance priors. Future extensions integrating differentiable pixel-wise spline optimization and automated synchrotron beamline feedback hooks can run `pyFAI-ZeroShot` inline as a zero-maintenance upstream stage feeding `pyFAI` integration servers.

## Materials and Methods

### Problem formulation and coordinate conventions
Given a 2D intensity array $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$, native detector pixel pitches $(p_1, p_2)$ in meters (row/column axes), and X-ray wavelength $\lambda$ (Å), the engine recovers the crystallographic phase $P$ from a phase library (built-in NIST SRM 660c `LaB6` with $a = 4.15682\text{ Å}$$^{16}$, `Si`, `Al2O3`, `CeO2`, `Quartz`, `AgBh`, `AgSt`, plus arbitrary user CIF/$d$-spacing tables via `custom_phase.py`) and the five active `pyFAI` geometry parameters $\Theta = (D, \mathrm{poni}_1, \mathrm{poni}_2, \mathrm{rot}_1, \mathrm{rot}_2)$ with $\mathrm{rot}_3 \equiv 0$. In all benchmark evaluations, beam-center distance error $\Delta C$ (px) is computed after converting both the predicted `.poni` geometry and the reference `.poni` file via `pyFAI`'s exact `AzimuthalIntegrator.getFit2D()` projection (`centerX`, `centerY`) so tilted PONI plane coordinates and beam intersections are compared consistently.

### Pre-processing and automated masking
Flat-panel negative pedestals are removed by `auto_offset` whenever the minimum finite pixel value satisfies $\min(I) < -0.05 \, P_{99.9}(I)$, subtracting $P_{1.0}(I)$ and clipping negatives to zero while leaving standard unsigned data untouched. Boolean invalid masks combine user masks (if supplied) with `auto_mask`: (i) `auto_gap_mask` detects horizontal or vertical constant-dark bands (`2–80 px` wide, line median $< 0.05 \times \text{global median}$ and band $P_{95} < 0.20 \times \text{global median}$ or $> 98\%$ non-positive pixels); (ii) isolated hot/bad pixels (`NaN`, `< 0`, `>= 1e9`, or $3\times 3$ spike outliers exceeding $\max(4\cdot \mathrm{nb}_{\max} + \max(15\sigma, 30), \mathrm{median} + 12\sigma)$); and (iii) central dark connected components within $0.08 \min(H, W)$ of the center representing beamstop shadows (`detect_beamstop_shadow`). Masks are block-pooled to coarse canvases by marking a block pixel invalid whenever $\ge 50\%$ of its constituent native pixels are masked.

### Multi-scale canvas ladder (`R2`) and radial peak extraction
`solve_image` starts on a `default` integer-factor block-averaged canvas with maximum dimension `CANVAS_MAX_SIDE = 520 px` (`canvas_of`). On this canvas, `ring_radii` azimuthally averages valid pixels about trial center $(c_x, c_y)$ into `NB = 2048` radial bins, locates peaks with `scipy.signal.find_peaks` (`prom = 0.05` of outer `r >= 25 px` maximum, minimum spacing `12` bins), refines positions via parabolic interpolation followed by five-parameter split-Gaussian fitting (`_split_gauss_center`, soft-L1 loss with analytical Jacobian) to resist asymmetric ring skew, and merges sub-peaks closer than `4.5 px`. An automated zero-prior escalation rule (`R2`) re-solves on a `large` canvas (`ceil(maxdim / 2)`, subject to area `<= 3.5 × 10^6` pixels) only if: (`a-none`) the default solve fails; (`b-rms-m`) `rms > 3.5 px` and `m < 3`; or (`c-risk`) `s_1 >= 5` and a lightweight no-cascade large-canvas probe deviates in distance by `|ΔD|/D >= 10%` (`FB_FORK = 0.10`), adopting the upgrade only when `(ps, m, -rms)` strictly improves (or when `c-risk` uncovers `>= 6` rings with `ps >= 0.45`, `rms < 1.5 px`, and `>= 2` additional rings). When solved geometry remains missing, under-determined (`m <= 3`), or low-scoring (`ps < 0.18`), a gated LLS-SNIP background-detrended pass$^{17}$ (`weak_ring.snip_background`, half-window `24` bins) rescues faint rings over steep diffuse scatter.

### Initializer cascade, guards, and phase arbitration
Within `solve_cascade`, a single-seed solve first runs from the geometric canvas center `geo`. `solver_core` computes the Layer-0 photometric sharpness center `center_by_sharpness` (coarse `46 px` grid plus closed-form radial-symmetry estimate `center_radial_symmetry` evaluated by background-subtracted profile peak contrast) and refines it via multi-ring algebraic Kåsa circle fitting (`kasa_center`, gated by residual `sigma < 1.0 px` and shift `< 30 px`). If the resulting anchor shifts by `> 8.0 px` (`far = True`), or if a ring-starvation check comparing `RS` against `geo` triggers (`|ln(D_RS / D_geo)| > D_RS_GATE = 0.46` or `m < 4`), `solve_cascade` unlocks multi-seed search `{geo, sh, RS, ray×(1.0, 2.0, 3.5)}`, ranking candidates by `consensus_pick` (counting votes within `2%` relative distance and `12 px` center tolerance). When off-axis distortion is indicated, an independent gradient-vote Hough center (`gradient_vote_center`) followed by iterative circle refinement (`refine_center_circles`) competes against geometric center.

For each library phase $P$, implied distances $D = r_p / \tan(2\theta_q)$ form `3%`-width vote clusters (`top-5` clusters with `>= 3` votes), refined via greedy bijective $(r_p, q)$ assignment across narrowing tolerances (`3% -> 2% -> 1%`) and nonlinear least squares over `(c_x, c_y, D)` with first-harmonic azimuthal re-centering (`_center_refine`). Candidate phases are arbitrated by `profile_score` $ps$ (Pearson correlation between measured radial spectrum and `sigma = 2.5 px` Gaussian phase template, keeping phases within `0.15` of $ps_{\max}$), coverage score `cov = m - 0.5(n_vis - m)`, subset-richness tie-breaking, and harmonic precedence (`AgBh` vs `AgSt`).

### High-resolution 2D, corner-tilt, and 2-parameter differential PV refinement
Winning solutions are promoted to high resolution according to azimuthal geometry:
1. **Full-ring native 2D Taubin circle fit (`refine_geometry_2d`):** When `ps > 0.4`, `m >= 4` (or `m = 3, ps >= 0.60`), and `s > 1`, intensity-weighted unbiased Taubin algebraic circle fits$^{18}$ are executed on the native image (or `2048 px` cap for `> 16 Mpx` frames) over complete rings (`r < 0.9 r_edge`), updating `(c_x, c_y, D)` via `1/r^2`-weighted zero-intercept regression (accepted if center shift `< 15 px` and `|ΔD|/D < 2.5%`).
2. **Regularized corner-arc sector tilt (`fit_tilt`):** When native 2D refinement skips a partial arc (`az_cov < 50%`), `fit_tilt` extracts up to `nsec = 72` azimuthal sector peaks on a `<= 1440 px` canvas (`min_gap = 14 px` guarding merged doublets, L2 tilt regularization `rot_reg = 0.05`, trust-region soft-L1 residual optimization) and accepts the update when `nrings >= 4`, `npts >= 40`, `rms5 < 0.6 mrad` (`< 6.0 mrad` on full rings), `tilt < 3.5°`, and `|ΔD|/D < 4%` (retaining 5 parameters if 5-param RMS improves by `>= 15%`, else 3 parameters).
3. **2-parameter differential Pseudo-Voigt decoupling (`_fit_pv_model`):** When neither native 2D nor sector tilt applies, high-resolution (`max_side = 1440` when `s > 2`, mapped via exact half-pixel pooling `cx_pv = (cx + 0.5)*sc - 0.5`) radial profiles are re-extracted and each matched peak is fitted to a six-parameter linear-background Pseudo-Voigt line shape (`peak_fit.py`). On partial arcs (`az_cov < 50%`, `n_rings >= 5`, `tan(2θ)` span `>= 0.05`), ordinary least squares solves:
$$r_i = D_{2\mathrm{p}} \tan(2\theta_i) + \delta r_0$$
subject to $|\delta r_0| \le 3 s_c$, $|D_{2\mathrm{p}} - D_0| / D_0 < 1.5\%$, $\mathrm{rms}_{2\mathrm{p}} < 0.70 \, \mathrm{rms}_{1\mathrm{p}}$, and $\mathrm{rms}_{2\mathrm{p}} < \min(\mathrm{rms}_{\mathrm{raw}}, \mathrm{rms}_{\mathrm{sol}})$. Using valid pixels across the active radial band $[0.8 r_{\min}, 1.1 r_{\max}]$, the mean radial unit vector $(\bar{u}_x, \bar{u}_y) = \langle (\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{c}) / r \rangle$ shifts the beam center by $\Delta \mathbf{c} = (\delta r_0 / (\bar{u}_x^2 + \bar{u}_y^2)) (\bar{u}_x, \bar{u}_y)$ whenever $\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}\|^2 \ge 0.25$. Otherwise, standard one-parameter Pseudo-Voigt distance scaling is applied.

### Uncertainty diagnostics and benchmark protocols
For every solution, `evaluate_confidence` reports `(m, ps, rms, az_cov)` alongside `cv_ratio = RMS_val / max(RMS_train, 0.20 px)` from even/odd-ring split-half extrapolation (`split_half_cv.py` for `m <= 5`), assigning tiers (`HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, `PARTIAL_ARC`, `LOW`). All DESY, CLS, ESRF, `silx_zoo`, and synthetic benchmarks were run strictly single-threaded/cold-start on CPU (Windows 11 x64, Python 3.13, `pyFAI 2025.12.1`) with `solver_core` caches cleared between frames. For the 24-frame `pyFAI` comparison (`P0` and `P1`), `pyFAI`'s `RingExtraction._calculate_num_of_points_to_keep` signature defect in `v2025.12.1` was identically patched across both arms (`_npk_fixed`), and control points were extracted via unweighted `cps.getList()`.
