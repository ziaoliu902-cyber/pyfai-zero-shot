## 二、英文正文（可直接发）

Subject: **Request for archived calibration time series (PONI drift) for benchmarking automatic geometry re-calibration**

Dear pyFAI team and D2AM staff,

I am a PhD student working on automatic geometry calibration of 2D X-ray
detectors from powder rings. Briefly, our pipeline pairs a classic
peak-position geometric solver (phase-library voting, iterative
ratio-tolerance peak-to-hkl matching, LSQ refinement with harmonic
re-centering) with a radial-symmetry center estimator and a regression
neural network as *one initializer among several*, arbitrated by a
cross-seed consensus gate. On the public MX CeO2 test images
(150–450 mm, Pilatus 6M) it recovers distances to 0.2 mm mean error in
~15 ms per frame, and on the 52-frame Al2O3 set it reproduces the
reference distance to 0.45 mm frame-to-frame MAD. A manuscript is in
preparation for J. Appl. Cryst., crediting the pyFAI test-image
repository and the D2AM beamline for these public frames.

I am writing to ask whether archived **calibration time series** could be
shared: sequences of calibrant frames (CeO2, LaB6, AgBh or similar)
acquired during a beamline session *together with the sequentially
refined PONIs*, i.e. data showing realistic slow geometry drift
(sample-to-detector distance, beam center, tilts) over minutes-to-hours.
Such data would let us benchmark **online self-calibration and automatic
drift re-locking**, which we see as the natural next step beyond the
single-frame calibration our paper covers.

Concretely, any subset that can be shared of:
1. calibrant frame sequences (even 20–100 frames) with timestamps;
2. per-refinement PONI files or the pyFAI calibration logs;
3. optionally, the raw calibrant images alone (we can re-refine them).

Provenance/anonymization is entirely at your discretion; we would of
course credit the beamline and, if the data contributes to a follow-up
publication, invite co-authorship. All our code and benchmark results
will be shared openly with the community.

Relatedly — if other instruments' calibration images (any detector,
any calibrant, with reference geometry) could be shared similarly, they
would feed a public **cross-instrument zero-shot calibration benchmark**
we are assembling from the pyFAI test-image collection; happy to share
the benchmark tables back for pyFAI's own regression testing.

Thank you for maintaining such a valuable public resource — the test-image
collection made this whole line of work possible.

Best regards,
